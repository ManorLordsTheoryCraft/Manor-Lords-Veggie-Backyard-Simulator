Simulation considerations
These are all the considerations made in the modelling of the simulation to make it as true to the game as possible.
Game Version. 0.8.104
VariableImpactHow it works in gameHow it is simulated
WorkersNeutralTwo backyard workers per family (Wife, Son). Husband never does backyard work. All three if family is a Smallholding.Exactly
TimeNeutralAt 1x speed, a calendar day ticks over every x seconds. It doesn't matter if a plant is sowed at the beginning or just before a day tickover, it will need a fixed number of ticks to be ready for harvest (see Datamined game values for the exact number, depending on the crop. Every worker action has an animation time in seconds that I managed to datamine from the game, including walking speed.Exactly
Plant Spot CyclingNeutralAn empty plant spot must be Plowed, only then can it be Sowed, only then can it Grow, only then can it be Harvested.Exactly
Task PriorityNeutralFrom highest priority to lowest: A worker holding a basket of veggie must go to the shed to drop it off. If there is a burgage chore (refuel/re-water) one backyard worker must step away from the backyard to do it. A plant spot that is ready to harvest must be harvested. A spot that is Plowed but not Sown must be Sown. A spot that is empty must be Plowed. If there are multiple plant spots at the same priority, the closest one will be matched to an available idle worker. Last and lowest priority is an outside job like the family's assigned job, guiding an ox, getting a drink, going to pray, etc.Exactly
No reassignment on dispatchNegativeA worker that is assigned a task some distance away will have to walk but will not be reassigned a nearer, closer, or higher priority task midwalk until the original assigned task is done (reserved on dispatch rule). This means if a worker who just Harvested the only plant in the vicinity which was ready to harvest in the moment and they should ideally stick around to wait for the others to ripen, they instead walk away to do Plowing and Sowing somewhere else, according to the Task Priority mechanic. Since Plowing and Sowing work are often far away from ripening spots, and workers don't know in advance if there are more that will ripen in succession if they just wait a few more seconds, this often leads to workers playing whack-a-mole and incurring lots of unnecessary walking. Also, if no more backyard tasks are available (e.g. all spots already Plowed, Sowed and Growing but none ready for Harvest), they become liable to be assigned to leave the plot for low priority outside tasks. Once assigned to leave, they will go do that first, even if some plant spots suddenly become available to Harvest a split second later.Exactly
Return loot to shedNegativeA worker doing a string of Harvests occasionally ends up with a basket in hand that they must return to the shed with to drop off. This was reintroduced in 0.8.078 and significantly impacts production.Exactly. See Golden Plants.
CloggingNegativeA veggie shed has capacity to hold only 1 person acting at a time. For large family plots, multiple workers may clog at the shed while waiting for the first one to drop their loot. Granary workers have a very long animation time when unloading a shed and can also clog it.Approximate. Backyard worker clogging themselves is simulated but the impact has been determined to be negligible. (except been for 4 family plots. Granary worker clogging is NOT SIMULATED.
Golden Plants & Veg Yield formulaEmergent, Probably NeutralNot every harvested plant leads to gaining 1 vegetable creating the basket in hand. Loot is gained via statistical mechanic determined at the time of backyard creation, assigning certain plant spots with Golden status (my terminology). Each spot's Golden status is fixed by the yield formula: Y (= number of plants * YieldMod * BaseYieldPer100Plants * 100, rounded) which decides how many plant spots are Golden, then symmetric index-1 dithering picks which spots exactly. This is on average identical to having a dice roll every time any particular spot is harvested like how some games do it, but the way Manor Lords does it significantly reduces CPU usage. Harvesting a Golden Plant sends that worker straight to the shed to drop the loot before any new task can be assigned.Exactly
Burgage choresNegativeBurgages need fuel monthly. When it needs refueling, a worker interrupts backyard work to walk to a fuel source to collect. In this simulation, leaving the front door and returning with fuel is modeled as "Time to Refuel", measured in days. It is the day the fuel arrives that dates the next request: the same day-of-month, in the next month. As a house chore, the request outranks every backyard task when competing for an available worker. Burgages also need water monthly, so a backyard worker needs to visit a well. Husband does not do house chores.available worker assigned to chore walks to the shed and waits there for Time to Refuel days, which has a reasonable preset number by default that the user can change. The first-ever chore day also has an adjustable preset. Both the walk and the wait are counted as idle, and the worker grid shows them as C on the idle color. Chores continue in the winter. Last checked for 0.8.104, the game keeps refuel day as a strict day-of-month number.
Calendar & Winter Plant Spot Utilization%EmergentA year is 365 days and February is always 28 days. The run starts on 01 Mar, so the first calendar year is simulated only from March onwards and is a partial year by design; partial years are left out of the averages. Within a year the last 90 days (01 Dec - 28 Feb) are Winter: no backyard labor allowed but burgage chores, but Growth continues. The output of a veggie plot is significantly impacted by how well the backyard is Sown to take advantage of the 90 day Grow-only period. I call this the Winter Plant Spot Utilization %. The exact relation is an emergent property and therefore must be simulated.Exactly
Field layoutEmergentEvery plant has 100 cm separation distance, with some jitter. The backyard has the plants split up into three planter beds with rows separating them approximately 500 cm wide for the middle splitter road and 300 cm for the horizontal splitter. The shed distance to the nearest plant appears to be related to the length of the plot. The veggie plot output is significantly impacted by how well the plant spots are distributed with respect to the location of the shed due to whack-a-mole effect and return-to-shed mechanics affecting total walk time. Clearly squarish backyards are optimal and long ones less so, but the exact total walk time vs plot shape is an emergent property and therefore must be simulated. Unfortunately I could not fully reverse-engineer the planter bed generation so it's just square and rectangle backyards for now.simulator in field layout Advanced Mode does 3 planter beds with the shed closest to the bed holding ~50% of the plants. The other two holding about 37% and 13%. User can set the width of the plot (Rows) and number of plants and let the plot grow in length, or choose Make Square mode (default) to attempt the simulator try to form a square backyard using the desired number of plants. I could not fully reverse-engineer the planter bed generation so it's just square and rectangle backyards for now.
Pathfinding and collisionsNegativeWorkers beeline to their intended location and bump/stall if there is another person in the way.Not simulated. Probably minor/not worth simulating.
Miscellaneous activityNegativeWorkers sometimes randomly stop to stretch their body or yawn, or if temporarily idle may go for a drink, or pray, which can have negative effects for output if backyard tasks are incoming.Not simulated. Probably minor/not worth simulating.
