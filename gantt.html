Simulation considerations
These are all the considerations made in the modelling of the simulation to make it as true to the game as possible.
Time. Labour is tracked in continuous real seconds; the calendar runs in day ticks of 40s. An action may straddle a tick edge — the worker never waits for one. Growth is tick based, not time based: a spot ripens on a tick boundary a fixed number of day ticks after the tick its sow finished in, so the sub-tick offset of the sow is discarded and the ripe edge always lands on a boundary.

The spot cycle. EMPTY → plow → PLOWED → sow → GROWING → (grow time) → RIPE → harvest → EMPTY. Growth is always the full grow time in day ticks on the game clock; only the wall-clock wait varies, by less than one tick, with the sow offset.

Workers. The Family(s) control shifts workers: two per family, three per family under Smallholding. Each worker carries its own clock, so several work different spots at the same time.

Task priority, highest first: the walk to drop loot, then the refuel house chore, then harvest, then sow, then plow. The drop-loot walk is not a queued choice at all: it is forced onto the worker that just harvested a Golden Plant, so nothing can outrank it. The other four are picked by taking the task that can start soonest; when two tasks can start at the same moment, priority decides, and a remaining tie is broken by the closest cell in Euclidean distance. Priority therefore only arbitrates between tasks available at the same moment, so a worker never sits idle while work exists. Walking is charged whenever a worker moves to another cell.

Golden Plants and the shed. Each spot's Golden status is fixed by the yield formula: the harvest yield (plants × YieldMod × BaseYieldPer100Plants ÷ 100, rounded) decides how many spots are Golden, and symmetric index-1 dithering picks which. Harvesting a Golden Plant sends that worker straight to the shed — which in the simulation is a spot about five plant's distance away from Plant Spot 1 — to drop loot before new work. The shed takes one drop at a time; more loot arrivals during an active drop have wait in place, which the simulation detects as Clogging. In the game this can happen if you have too many workers finding loot and going to the shed at the same time (4 family, smallholding, large plot, many granary workers trying to retrieve loot from pantry etc). Granary workers are NOT modelled.

House chore: refueling. Burgages need fuel monthly. When it needs refueling, a worker interrupts backyard work to walk to a fuel source to collect. In this simulation, leaving the front door and returning with fuel is modeled as "Time to Refuel", measured in days. It is the day the fuel arrives that dates the next request: the same day-of-month, in the next month. As a house chore, the request outranks every backyard task when it competes for the same worker at the same moment; only the walk to drop loot sits above it, and that one is forced onto the worker that just harvested. In this simulation, the chosen worker walks to the shed and waits there for Time to Refuel days. Both the walk and the wait are counted as idle, never as work, and the worker grid shows them as C on the idle color. Refuel chore continues in the winter. As of 0.8.104 the game keeps refuel day as a strict day-of-month number with no handling for a day the next month does not have, so a short month is skipped rather than clamped or rolled over. E.g. a 31st refuel waits for the next 31-day month (February, April, June, September and November are passed over), and a 29th, 30th or 31st in January goes straight to March. This bug is also modeled in the simulation.

Calendar. A year is 365 days and February is always 28 days. The run starts on 01 Mar, so the first calendar year is simulated only from March onwards and is a partial year by design; partial years are left out of the averages. Within a year the last 90 days (01 Dec – 28 Feb) are winter: no labour, but growth continues, so autumn crops ripen and wait for spring. Winter is excluded from all percentages and idle reporting.

Field layout. A near-square grid filled row-first from the bottom-left: the index runs left to right along each row, then the next row up. No snake fill. Walking is between cell centres, never by spot index. The shed square sits at the same lattice level.

Crop yield, not harvest count. The simulation counts harvest actions. To get the vegetable count, multiply by YieldMod and BaseYieldPer100Plants, divide by 100, and round; the per-year table shows both.

The ceilings. Averaged Worker Output is measured from the run, over complete non-Y1 years. There is no formula-derived ceiling anywhere on this page. The Find limit sweep answers the same question by measurement instead: it runs the whole setup again across a range of plant counts and reports the best output it finds, so a ceiling here always comes from a run that actually happened.

Datamined values. See the Datamined game values section below. It holds the game's clock and labour constants — Day Tick Duration, Plow, Sow, Harvest, Drop Loot and Grow time in day ticks — the movement constants, Walk speed and cm per grid cell, and BaseYieldPer100Plants for each crop. Everything else in this page (spot counts, workers, YieldMod, Time to Refuel) is a modelling choice rather than a game value. The calendar is the exception: its year length, its 28-day February and its winter span are faithful to the game.

Not modelled: worker pathfinding or collisions, tool quality, weather, stamina, and the shed's capacity as a logistics node beyond the single-drop constraint already described. Two workers may briefly converge on the same square since there is no collision model.

Miscellaneous activity, also not modelled: water collection from water wells, workers randomly stopping work to stretch their body or yawn, going for a drink, or going to pray. These are left out because they are either lower priority tasks than backyard work or cost very little time, or both.
